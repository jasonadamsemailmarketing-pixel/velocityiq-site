The Problem

Every pipeline eventually fills up with hope dressed as fact

A rep needs the number, so a maybe becomes a yes. Worse: an AI model asked to score a lead's budget from nothing but a company domain will invent one anyway, because that's what language models do when a prompt demands an answer. RevGuard was built specifically to close that gap.

Before vs. After

Without RevGuard

  • Budget and Authority scores inferred by AI from a bare company domain — effectively invented
  • Deals advance stages based on rep judgment, not confirmed data
  • Duplicate leads pile up silently across the CRM
  • Stalled deals sit untouched with no one alerted
  • Bad-domain or malformed submissions pollute the pipeline

With RevGuard

  • Budget and Authority scored from what the lead actually stated — a lookup, not a guess
  • A deal cannot advance without meeting its exit criteria
  • Daily duplicate check flags matches by domain + normalized company name
  • Daily stalled-deal check alerts the sales manager automatically
  • Invalid submissions are quarantined before they ever reach the CRM
System Design

Five automated checkpoints, one accountable pipeline

Each tool handles what it does best. Make.com owns intake and validation; Pipedream owns scoring and CRM writes, where real code replaces fragile drag-and-drop formulas.

1
Tally.so → Make.com

Intake & Validation

A lead submits the form; Make.com checks the email domain against free consumer providers (gmail, yahoo, outlook, hotmail). Anything that fails is quarantined to a Google Sheet with a Slack alert — never silently dropped.

2
Pipedream → Groq

BANT Scoring

Budget and Authority are scored from the lead's own stated answers via a direct lookup table — no AI guessing involved. Need and Timeline are read by Groq's Llama 3.3 model, but only from what the lead actually wrote.

3
Pipedream → HubSpot

Qualification Gate

Total ≥70 creates a contact and a deal in the Qualification stage. 40–69 creates a contact routed to Nurture. Below 40, the contact is logged and flagged — disqualified, not discarded.

4
Pipedream, daily

Stalled Deal Detection

A scheduled workflow checks every open deal's last-activity date. Anything untouched 7+ days triggers a Slack alert to the sales manager — before the deal quietly dies.

5
Pipedream, daily

Duplicate Detection

A second scheduled workflow matches contacts on email domain plus a normalized company name. Matches are flagged for human review — never auto-merged, since a wrong merge is worse than a visible duplicate.

The Rubric

BANT scoring, in full

The part most conceptual designs leave as a black box. Here's exactly what RevGuard checks.

DimensionScored fromPoints
BudgetStated budget range — direct lookup: $50k+ = 25, $10k–$50k = 15, Not sure yet = 50–25
AuthorityStated decision role — direct lookup: I decide = 25, I influence = 15, Just researching = 50–25
NeedGroq reads the stated problem for specificity and urgency0–25
TimelineGroq reads the stated purchase timeframe0–25

≥70 total = Auto-Qualified, deal created. 40–69 = Nurture, contact only. <40 = Disqualified, logged with reason. No dimension is ever left to guesswork.

Engineering Decisions

Why it's built this way

Why Budget and Authority aren't AI-scored

An early version asked the model to infer budget from a company domain alone. It complied — by inventing a number. That's the exact failure this system exists to prevent, so both fields now come from a direct lookup against what the lead actually stated.

Why scoring logic moved to Pipedream, not Make.com

Make.com's drag-and-drop filter formulas repeatedly mis-evaluated a four-value sum. Moving the math into real Node.js code in Pipedream made the logic testable, debuggable, and reliable — the right tool for arithmetic and conditional branching.

Why duplicates are flagged, not auto-merged

At this tier, there's no paid tool's audit trail to safely undo a bad automatic merge. Flagging for a human to review costs a little time; a wrong auto-merge destroys data. The safer default wins.

Why this runs across two Pipedream accounts

Pipedream's free tier caps active deployed workflows at three per account. With another portfolio project already using three slots, RevGuard runs in a second free account rather than upgrading — a real, documented constraint of building on genuinely free tools.
